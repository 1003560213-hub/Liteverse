## Liteverse 0.6.0 — Developer Preview

Liteverse 0.6.0 changes how a large library becomes useful. Every imported
paper is now read, summarized with verbatim key points, and connected to the
rest of your library on your Mac within seconds, without waiting for an AI
review. The universe is redrawn as a real 3D deep field built from Blender, and
the interface was rebuilt for laptops.

### Read hundreds of papers faster

- **Key points in seconds, no AI required.** Each paper gets an abstract, up to
  eight key sentences (question, method, result, limitation, assumption),
  quantities, and a parsed reference list. Every key point is a verbatim
  quotation with its page number, so nothing is invented. They are clearly
  labelled *Extracted · unreviewed*.
- **Citation links between your papers.** Reference lists are parsed and
  matched to the papers you already have by arXiv identifier or DOI (exact) or
  by author, year, and journal (probable). The Inspector shows the sentence in
  which one paper cites the other. A citation is a bibliographic fact, not a
  claim of agreement.
- **Automatic regions and galaxies.** Unreviewed papers are grouped by shared
  references (bibliographic coupling), co-citation, and shared vocabulary
  using Leiden community detection. Three organizing principles are offered in
  Settings → Layout. Reviewed regions never move.
- **Desk for fast reading.** A keyboard-driven Triage list (J/K, Enter, O to
  open the PDF at the key point), a Galaxy brief that orders papers so that
  cited work comes first, and a Compare matrix of verbatim sentences side by
  side.
- **Open the PDF at the quote.** Page links open a native PDF viewer at the
  exact page with the quoted sentence highlighted.
- **Apple Intelligence summaries (optional).** On a Mac with macOS 26 and Apple
  Intelligence enabled, Liteverse can write a short on-device summary of a paper
  from its verbatim key points. Nothing leaves your Mac. Summaries are labelled
  as drafts and never become evidence or change reviewed cards.
- **AI review by galaxy.** For AI-assisted curation, `liteverse digest packet`
  prepares one galaxy at a time, and `liteverse digest apply` accepts only
  answers whose every statement cites the extracted quotations of the right
  papers. Relations from this step stay candidates until original-page review.
- **Parallel local preparation.** Several PDFs are read at once, a stuck PDF is
  stopped after five minutes, and failed items can be retried together.

### A real Deep Universe

- **3D galaxies from Blender.** Ten galaxy types (grand-design, barred and
  flocculent spirals, elliptical, lenticular, ring, irregular dwarf, interacting
  pair, starburst, Seyfert) are generated in Blender from exponential discs,
  Sérsic bulges, logarithmic spiral arms, dust lanes, and star-forming knots,
  and rendered as point clouds with WebGL. The flat PNG artwork is gone.
- **A deep field behind everything**, with thousands of faint distant galaxies
  and foreground stars with diffraction spikes.
- **Honest visual encoding.** Regions are galaxy clusters with faint
  intracluster light; research notes live in the accretion disc of the central
  black hole; papers are stars placed by how often your library cites them
  (foundational papers near the bulge) and by year along the arms. Colour lenses
  show evidence tier, research heat, publication year, or citation centrality.
- **Galaxies keep their surface brightness** at every distance and level of
  detail, and rotate slowly with a flat rotation curve when motion is allowed.

### Built for laptops

- **Draws only when something changes.** No frames are rendered while the
  universe is still. On battery, in Low Power Mode, when the Mac is hot, or
  when the window is covered, Liteverse drops to a power-saving quality and
  stops ambient motion.
- **Trackpad-native:** pinch to zoom toward the cursor, two-finger scroll to
  pan, drag or rotate to orbit, ⌘-scroll to zoom with a mouse.
- **A layout for 13-inch screens:** a compact toolbar, a collapsible Atlas
  outline on the left, an Inspector on the right that resizes the view instead
  of covering it, and a status strip. The window remembers its size and
  supports full screen.
- **Mac menus and shortcuts:** ⌘O import, ⌘K search, ⌘1/⌘2 Sky/Desk, ⌘[ back,
  ⌘, settings, ⌃⌘S Atlas, ⌥⌘I Inspector.

### Simpler

- One search (⌘K) over titles, abstracts, key points, galaxies, and commands.
- One project switcher, one way back (breadcrumb, ⌘[, Escape), one lens menu.
- Settings now has five clear tabs: Library, Project, Layout, Intelligence,
  and Display.
- Removed: the unused web/Cloudflare build and its dependencies, the in-app
  context preview and its unread request queue, duplicate search and
  catalogue-sync paths, the zoom slider, and the 2D sticker artwork (about 48
  MB).

### Verification

- TypeScript, ESLint, public-source privacy checks, and the automated suite
  pass, including new tests for the Tier-0 extraction and clustering, the
  sky and library models, the Blender asset manifest, and the native
  contracts.
- The interface was exercised end to end in headless Chromium with a fictional
  demonstration library.
- The macOS app, Local Worker, and Apple Intelligence helper are compiled and
  signed on the macOS 26 GitHub runner.

### Known limitations

- Key points and reference parsing work from PDF text. Two-column layouts,
  running headers, equations, and scanned pages reduce their quality; such
  papers are flagged. OCR is not yet included.
- Probable citation matches can be wrong and are shown as probable.
- Apple Intelligence summaries need macOS 26, an Apple Intelligence–capable
  Mac, and Apple Intelligence turned on. The on-device model reads only a short
  context, so it sees the key points, not the whole paper.
- Restoring a verified backup still stores it in the recovery area; switching
  the active workspace to it remains manual.
- The download is ad-hoc signed and not notarized by Apple.

### Install

Requires macOS 13 or later on an Apple Silicon Mac (Apple Intelligence
summaries: macOS 26). Unzip, move `Liteverse.app` to Applications, then
Control-click it and choose **Open** on first launch.
